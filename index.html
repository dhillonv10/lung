How the gain metric is derived: The gain is the ratio of a regimen’s expected PFS to osimertinib’s expected PFS in the same risk profile — approximately the reciprocal of the subgroup hazard ratio under proportional hazards (gain ≈ 1/HR). At the ITT level, using each trial’s own contemporary control arm as the denominator, the combination-over-monotherapy effect is ~1.53× for osi+chemo (25.5/16.7) and ~1.43× for amiva+lazer (23.7/16.6). (The earlier external FLAURA control of 18.9 months understated this baseline, giving only ~1.35× and ~1.25×.) This baseline gain applies to all patients. When the displayed gain exceeds it, the combination is disproportionately benefiting this profile by overcoming adverse prognostic biology. Worked example (liver metastases): osimertinib median ~11.0 months (Felip 2024); amivantamab-lazertinib reaches ~18.2 months within MARIPOSA, so 18.2/11.0 ≈ 1.65×; the FLAURA2 liver-mets HR of ~0.60 implies ~1/0.60 ≈ 1.67× for osi+chemo (a cross-trial estimate, to be read with caution). For favorable-risk patients, gains near the baseline suggest the benefit is primarily the general treatment effect, which must be weighed against the added toxicity.

Methodology — Ratio-Referenced Estimates
Efficacy is expressed as fold-change (×) relative to each trial’s ITT median PFS, derived from published subgroup hazard ratios and medians; ratios <1.0× indicate shorter expected PFS. The gain-versus-monotherapy metric uses each trial’s own contemporary control arm (16.7 mo FLAURA2, 16.6 mo MARIPOSA) as the osimertinib denominator rather than the external FLAURA ITT median (18.9 mo). Correlated factors are grouped into Metastatic Burden (brain and/or liver) and Molecular Risk (TP53 and/or detectable ctDNA) domains to mitigate collinearity; severity modifiers within a domain (liver, or dual TP53+ctDNA) can raise a single-domain profile to high risk. EGFR mutation subtype (Ex19del vs L858R) is treated as a minor prognostic modifier applied equally across all three regimens — it shifts absolute PFS estimates but, because no EGFR-by-factor interaction is modeled, leaves the gain-vs-monotherapy ratio unchanged. Median PFS is used as the anchor because it is the primary endpoint reported uniformly across FLAURA, FLAURA2 and MARIPOSA and the outcome for which subgroup estimates are consistently available; it does not capture the tail/plateau of the survival curve and is not a perfect surrogate for OS, which is shown alongside.

⚠ These grade ≥3 rates are not directly comparableThe FLAURA2 figures above are any-cause grade ≥3 AEs (final analysis); the MARIPOSA figure is treatment-related grade ≥3 AEs. Definitions, follow-up, and populations differ, and no head-to-head trial has compared osimertinib-chemotherapy with amivantamab-lazertinib. These within-trial rates describe each regimen against its own control and should not be read as evidence that one combination is more toxic than the other.
ⓘ Subcutaneous Amivantamab, Thromboprophylaxis, and the Amiva+Lazer Toxicity Profile The AE rates shown above for amivantamab-lazertinib are from MARIPOSA, which used intravenous (IV) amivantamab without routinely mandated thromboprophylaxis. Two subsequent developments reduce this burden, and they act through different mechanisms:

(1) Prophylactic anticoagulation. Direct oral anticoagulant prophylaxis for the first ~4 months is now recommended. In PALOMA-3, 81% of patients received prophylaxis, and VTE occurred in 10% of those who did versus 21% of those who did not.
(2) The subcutaneous (SC) formulation, compared head-to-head against IV in PALOMA-3 (both arms receiving prophylaxis): infusion-related reactions fell roughly 5-fold (13% SC vs 66% IV), VTE was 9% (SC) vs 14% (IV), and administration time fell from up to 5 hours to under 5 minutes.

The MARIPOSA VTE rate of 37% therefore reflects IV administration without routine prophylaxis; with prophylactic anticoagulation and/or the SC formulation, VTE is substantially lower. Clinicians should factor local SC availability and anticoagulation practice into regimen selection. (Leighl NB et al., JCO 2024; PALOMA-3)

Osimertinib Monotherapy

Steady-state, low-intensity toxicity pattern

Toxicity is generally continuous and low-grade. Diarrhea and skin AEs predominate but rarely exceed Grade 2. No marked induction-phase spike. ILD/pneumonitis (~4%) can occur at any time and requires immediate drug interruption. QTc prolongation and LVEF changes require periodic monitoring but are uncommon.

Patient experience: Most patients tolerate indefinitely with minimal QoL impact. FLAURA PROs showed global health improvement maintained through treatment.

Osimertinib + Platinum-Pemetrexed

Front-loaded hematologic toxicity, attenuated maintenance phase

Months 0–3 (Platinum Induction): Peak toxicity. Cytopenias (anemia 20% Gr3, neutropenia 14% Gr3-4), nausea (43%), fatigue. G-CSF may be required. Grade 4 events exclusively hematologic.

Months 3+ (Pemetrexed Maintenance): Grade ≥3 AE onset reduced ~50%. Residual toxicity primarily pemetrexed (fatigue, cytopenias) + ongoing osimertinib. 43% ultimately discontinue pemetrexed; treatment continues with osi alone.

Patient experience: QoL global health scores showed greater improvement with osi mono vs combination, though the difference did not meet clinically meaningful deterioration threshold.

Amivantamab + Lazertinib

Early multi-domain toxicity with dose-optimization and stabilization

Months 0–4 (Active Management): Highest burden. IRRs (63%, mostly first infusion), skin/nail onset (~14 days), VTE (median onset ~84 days), dose titration. Dose interruptions ~64%, reductions ~36% — expected and did not compromise PFS in exploratory analyses.

Months 4+ (Stabilization): New-onset AEs decrease substantially. VTE prophylaxis can be discontinued. Skin management becomes routine. Peripheral edema (MET-related) may accumulate but is manageable.

Mitigation: The COCOON prophylactic dermatologic regimen reduced Grade ≥2 skin AEs from 76.5% to 38.6% at week 12 (see the Practical tab for the full protocol). In PALOMA-3, SC amivantamab reduced IRRs ~5-fold (13% vs 66%) and administration time from up to 5 hrs to <5 min; VTE was 9% (SC) vs 14% (IV), both with prophylaxis, versus 37% in MARIPOSA (IV, no routine prophylaxis).

Choosing Between Combinations

Favoring Osi + Chemo: Patients with brain metastases at baseline (systemic PFS HR 0.47; intracranial PFS HR 0.58 in the CNS-mets subgroup); limited long-term infusion access (chemo is front-loaded); familiarity with chemo workflows; settings without SC amivantamab availability.

Favoring Amiva + Lazer: Patient preference to avoid chemo/cytopenias; high-risk molecular profile (dual EGFR+MET inhibition); SC amivantamab available; younger patient valuing long-term control (PFS plateau signal); platinum contraindication.

Favoring Osi Mono: Elderly/frail; QoL priority; comorbidities increasing toxicity risk; limited social support; favorable-risk profile where combo gain is modest relative to added toxicity.

No head-to-head trial exists between osi+chemo and amiva+lazer. Cross-trial comparisons are hypothesis-generating only.

⚠ Model Limitations This tool is a decision-support and risk-stratification framework, not a validated predictive algorithm or a patient-level nomogram. (1) The variables used are established markers of prognosis; using them to guide treatment intensification assumes they are also predictive of differential benefit — a hypothesis supported by larger subgroup effects (e.g., TP53-mutant HR 0.65 vs wild-type 0.75; liver 0.58; CNS systemic PFS 0.47) but not confirmed, as these subgroups were not powered for interaction and interaction tests were not significant. (2) The domain grouping and severity hierarchy are transparent heuristics, not empirically rank-ordered against patient-level data; different high-risk combinations that reach the same category are treated equivalently. (3) Established prognostic factors not captured here — ECOG performance status and weight loss in particular — are omitted, partly because the pivotal trials enrolled only ECOG PS 0–1, limiting the source-data range. (4) All subgroup estimates are cross-trial and exploratory; without patient-level data, absolute estimates should be read as population-level associations for discussion, not individual predictions.
